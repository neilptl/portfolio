Explainable AI: trust needs the score and the reason

Large language models now help with decisions in health care, finance, and law. Tests show how often a model is right. Explanations show the reason behind each answer. Trust needs both.

Definition and stakes

What explainable AI means, and why it matters

Explainable AI gives people the reasons behind a model’s answer.

Transparency lets users check those reasons, catch errors, and challenge unfair results. Without it, people must trust a model on faith.

NIST’s four principles of a good explanation1

ExplanationThe system gives a reason for each output.
MeaningfulThe intended user can follow the reason.
Explanation accuracyThe reason reflects the system’s real process.
Knowledge limitsThe system flags cases outside its design.

Why transparency matters: four real cases

Health care

17.7% → 46.5%

A widely used care algorithm treated past spending as a sign of medical need. Correcting it raised the share of Black patients flagged for extra care from 17.7% to 46.5%.2

Finance

Specific reasons

U.S. lenders must state specific reasons for every credit denial. A 2022 CFPB circular applies this rule to complex algorithms.3

Law

$5,000 fine

In June 2023, a federal judge fined two New York lawyers and their firm. Their brief cited six court cases invented by ChatGPT.4

Regulation

€35M or 7%

The EU AI Act took effect in August 2024. Its rules for general-purpose AI began on August 2, 2025. Top fines reach €35 million or 7% of global turnover.5

Challenges in explainability

Why large language models are hard to explain

405 billion

Model complexity

Llama 3.1 405B spreads every answer across 405 billion parameters in 126 layers. No single part holds the reason.6

1 neuron, 4 topics

Tangled concepts

In a small test model, one neuron fired on citations, English dialogue, web requests, and Korean text. Researchers call this superposition.7

25% and 39%

Explanations after the fact

Anthropic hid answer hints in test questions. Claude 3.7 Sonnet admitted to using the hint in 25% of cases. DeepSeek R1 admitted it in 39%.8

3 to 6×

Bias in the data

In 2023 tests, four LLMs picked gender-stereotyped jobs 3 to 6 times more often than other jobs. Many of their stated reasons were factually wrong.9

37 out of 100

Hidden training data

Stanford’s 2023 transparency index scored 10 major developers. The average was 37 out of 100. The top score was 54.10

No shared standard

Rules ahead of methods

The EU AI Act and U.S. lending law demand explanations. No agreed test yet defines a good explanation for an LLM.3,5

Validation and performance metrics

How validation and metrics build trust

Validation tests a model on data it never saw in training. Metrics turn each test into a number people can compare and track over time.

  1. Hold out dataKeep a test set out of training.
  2. BenchmarkScore standard tests, such as MMLU’s 57 subjects.11
  3. Stress testTry odd inputs, attacks, and red-team prompts.
  4. Check the reasonsTest whether explanations match real behavior.14
  5. MonitorTrack errors and drift after launch.
What each metric measures
MetricWhat it measures
AccuracyShare of answers that are correct.
Precision and recallFalse alarms versus missed cases.
CalibrationMatch between stated confidence and the real hit rate.
TruthfulnessResistance to common myths and false claims.12
FairnessEqual results across gender, race, and age groups.
FaithfulnessMatch between an explanation and the real cause of the output.14

Current techniques

What leading AI labs are doing

OpenAI

  • 2023 GPT-4 wrote explanations for all 307,200 neurons in GPT-2. About 1,000 scored 0.8 or higher.17
  • 2024 A sparse autoencoder found 16 million features inside GPT-4.18
  • Its o-series reasoning models show summaries of their thinking.

Anthropic

  • 2024 Mapped 34 million features in Claude 3 Sonnet, including one for the Golden Gate Bridge.19
  • 2025 Circuit tracing showed Claude 3.5 Haiku planning a rhyme before writing the line.20
  • 2025 Released its tracing tools as open source.21

Google DeepMind

  • 2024 Gemma Scope released over 400 open sparse autoencoders for Gemma 2.22
  • The Learning Interpretability Tool shows attention and word-importance views.23
  • Google researchers introduced model cards in 2019.24

Meta

  • Open Llama weights let outside teams inspect model internals.6
  • The Captum library scores how much each input word drove an output.25
  • Llama model cards list training data size, GPU hours, and emissions.27

A shared warning. In July 2025, 41 researchers from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, and other groups asked labs to keep model reasoning readable and monitored.26
